**Read when:** Building or changing a deck, a slide layout, a deck element, a slide-owned question set, the player or the editor. For the shape and house style of a whole deck, also read `docs/design/LESSON_STYLE.md`.

A **deck** is one combined, directed lesson for the projector, played at `/decks/<id>`. It is also a small slide editor, a stripped-down PowerPoint around the site's own pieces: a teacher can refine a deck Claude built, add slides to it, or build their own. Decks wrap what the site already has (generators, authored question sets, boards), so a lesson stays in one place; tool pages are unchanged. Decks are the intended successor to the per-tool Teach deck and Lessons modes. Plan: `docs/pathways/decks.md`.

## Published decks (`published.ts`, `AdminGate.tsx`, `api/decks.ts`)

- **Publishing** is a button in the editor's File menu (**Publish this deck**, **Publish again**, **Unpublish**; `npm run deck -- publish <id>` does the same from the command line). It sends a frozen copy of the deck, with its questions embedded, to the `maths.published_decks` table (the frozen copy and its listing entry together; the two public GETs are cached for a minute); the working copy is untouched and later edits change only the working copy until it is published again.
- **Reading is open to everyone** (`GET /api/decks?published` and `?published=<id>`); everything else on `/api/decks` still needs the admin key. The Decks page and the read-only player read only the published copies, never the working copy or this device's saved edits. <!-- ref -->
- **The password** is the Developing-tools password and the same switch: entering it at the editor's prompt turns Developing-tools mode on, and turning that off locks the editor again. It is a light check, not security: the string is in the site's code (`src/devAccess.ts`).
- Published decks come from the website, so the local dev server and an offline device show "can't be listed here". `npm run smoke:published` checks the whole flow with the service simulated. <!-- ref -->

## The model (`src/decks/types.ts`)

- A **deck** is `{ id, title, summary, tool?, toolPath?, slides }`.
- A **slide** is `{ id, family, title, brief?, els }` (`brief` is the optional slide brief; older decks' `intent` / `questionBrief` are folded into it by `withBriefs` when the editor opens a deck): a canvas `CANVAS_W × CANVAS_H` (1600 × 900) units, scaled to fit. The family sets its accent bar (`FAMILY_STYLE`; none is green, yellow or red, the level colours); the title names it in the jump list and the slide strip.
- An **element** is a box (`id, x, y, w, h` in canvas units; a line runs from `(x, y)` to `(x + w, y + h)`, so its `w`/`h` may be negative) and one of:

| `kind` | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `text` | Text, `$…$` inline maths, a new line per line | `size` (≥ `MIN_TEXT` = 26), `bold`, `colour`, `align`, `middle` |
| `shape` | Rectangle or ellipse | `fill`, `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `radius` |
| `line` | Line or arrow | `colour`, `width`, `arrow` |
| `image` | A picture, stored as a data URL | Large images are scaled to the canvas and saved as JPEG on upload |
| `maths` | An equation on its own | `latex`, `size`, `colour`, `align`; made and changed in the equation editor |
| `ratioTable` | The site's ratio table (`RatioTable`): quantities as columns, a row per scale step, the factor between two rows as an arrow outside the table | `table` (`headers`, `rows`, `operations`: one LaTeX factor such as `\times 2` between each pair of rows, `given`), `fill` as a board (Structure only keeps the headings and the given rows and hides the arrows; empty cells draw as "?"), `size` (cell text size), `staged` (one more row, with its arrow, per click). The table is laid out at its **full size** from the first click, with later rows hidden in place (`hideFrom`), and fitted to its box once from the whole table, so a four- or five-step table never rescales or moves as a row arrives | <!-- ref -->
| `numberGrid` | The 1 to 100 grid (`NumberGrid.tsx`, logic in `src/shared/numberGrid.ts`): **Structure only** (default) is the plain numbers, tapped to scribble in the chosen prime's colour (2 blue, 3 green, 5 orange, 7 purple; the prime circled) with **Finish the n times table** completing the rest while keeping tapped marks; **Filled in** is the finished sieve; **Blank** an empty grid. Marks are hand-drawn scribbles seeded by the number, not coloured cells. Taps are live in the player and are not saved. | optional `fill`, `staged` (one click per sieve step: 1 crossed out, then each prime in `stages`, default 2, 3, 5, 7), `stages`. The player's top bar has **Print grids** (the `hundredSquareSheet()` printable, two 1 to 100 grids per A4) on any slide that has one |
| `venn` | A Venn diagram (`Venn.tsx`, geometry in `vennLayout.ts`, logic in `src/shared/venn.ts`): a box (optional label), two circles with rule labels and **cards**: words, numbers or **prime tiles** (coloured squares in the number grid's prime colours). A card with a `region` (`left`, `middle`, `right`) is set out in that part of the diagram; a card without one starts scattered round the outside (corners, margins, bottom). In the player every card drags with a finger, stylus or mouse and stays where it is dropped: no checking, no tick, no reset button, live only and not saved (leaving the slide puts them back). The drag stops the phone swipe (`data-no-swipe`); with the Pen on, the pen takes the touch. The editor's **Venn options** has a quick "prime tiles for two numbers" (placed or scattered) | `left`, `right` (circle labels), optional `box`, `cards` (`{ text, tile?, region? }`), `staged` (one click per card), `lines` (`hcf` and/or `lcm`: the answer line from the placed prime tiles, one click each after the cards; `hcfLine` / `lcmLine`), `labelScale` (the box and circle labels only, so a Venn drawn larger keeps its labels: the `wide` layout option of `vennSlide` uses the right-hand space and sets it), `size` (card scale, 1 by default; also scales the labels and lines for a small Venn). **Build mode** (`listing`, `palette`; layout `vennBuildSlide`, `S.vennBuild`): `listing` draws the numbers as rows of prime tiles (`10 = [2][5]`) in the left two fifths, the Venn takes the right three fifths, and `palette` draws prime tile buttons along the bottom: each press adds one more of that tile to the diagram (as many as needed), to drag where it goes; dragged back down onto the buttons it goes away. Live only, never saved, nothing checked (the editor's Venn options has both lists). Layouts: `vennSlide` (full slide), `vennBuildSlide` and `vennRowSlide` (four small Venns in a row, a to d, `size` 1.5). The deck check fails prime tiles that are not where the two numbers' factors go (`tileProblems`), and warns about an answer line with nothing to multiply or more than 12 scattered cards | <!-- ref -->
| `visualiser` | A full-width box that pulls the document camera (the visualiser) through: in the player it starts the camera when the slide opens (`WhiteboardWorkspace` with `autoStart`); the workspace options menu stops it or picks another camera. Nothing is drawn in the editor or in previews. Layout `visualiserSlide` (`S.visualiser`) | none <!-- ref --> |
| `factorTree` | A factor tree (`FactorTree.tsx`, logic in `src/shared/factorTree.ts`): a number splits in two (both bigger than 1, so no split uses 1) until every branch ends in a prime, which is circled in its colour (2 blue, 3 green, 5 orange, 7 purple, one neutral ring for 11 and above) and stops; primality is computed from the number, so a wrong circle cannot be authored. The whole tree is laid out at its full size from the first click | `tree` (`{ n, kids?: [node, node], given? }`; `treeProblems` checks it), `fill` (Filled in the whole tree; Structure only the root, the numbers marked `given`, the numbers above them and "?" where the next ones go; Blank the root alone), `staged` (one click per split, in reading order, top row first then left to right), `reveal` (clicks after the last split, in order: `product` "n = 2 × 2 × 5", `counts` the tally of prime and how many, `index` the index column and the product in index form; Filled in only), `strip` (Primes to 20 under the tree), `rules` (the divisibility reminder under it). The editor starts a tree with `autoTree(n)` (splits off the smallest prime) |
| `inverseTrig` | An animated graph (`InverseTrig.tsx`, geometry in `src/shared/inverseTrig.ts`): y = sin x (restricted to −π/2 ≤ x ≤ π/2), cos x (0 ≤ x ≤ π) or tan x (−π/2 < x < π/2, drawn between dashed asymptotes that fold into the horizontal ones of arctan), reflected in y = x and separated into its own axes as the inverse. Four clicks after the first view (the whole sine curve): **restrict** (the rest fades, the kept part is bold with open end points and its domain and range marked), **mirror line** (y = x), **fold** (a copy swings over the line, every point swapping x and y, so it is exactly y = arcsin x), **separate** (the copy slides out to a second set of axes and the first set moves left). The click plays as a short animation in the player (Back plays it in reverse); the editor, thumbnails and print draw the finished state, and reduced-motion jumps straight to each state. Both axes carry ±1, ±π/2 and ±π, at the same scale on x and y so the reflection is a true one | `fn` (`"sin"`, `"cos"` or `"tan"`; the details of each are `INVERSE_SPECS`). Layout `inverseTrigSlide` (`S.inverseTrig(id, fn)`) |
| `ink` | Hand-writing on a slide, drawn with the editor's **Pen**: one full-slide layer per slide (`x 0, y 0`, the whole canvas), strokes `{c, w, p}` (colour, width in slide units, flat points) in slide units, drawn smoothed by `InkLayer.tsx`. Not selectable on the slide; **palm rejection**: with the Pen on, a touch that is large (over 32px) or lands within 0.8 s of the stylus touching or hovering is swallowed by the editor's stage (`isPalm` in `Editor.tsx`), and no touch reaches the box-select or deselect underneath (the Pen toolbar has **Rub out** (whole lines you touch, `inkMath.ts`), **Clear** and **Done**; Esc stops); undo works as for any edit; it merges like any element and shows in the player, thumbnails and exports | `strokes` |
| `bar` | The site's bar model (`BarModel`, `src/shared/barModel.ts`) in two looks: the **ratio look** (`rows`, the stacked layout Ratio Sharing uses: a row per quantity aligned from the left, white boxes with a navy border, a value in each box, "= total" at the end, purple extra boxes for a difference, a brace across the rows) and a **single bar** of coloured parts with braces above or below (for fractions) | `bar` (`rows`: name, units, `unitLabel`, `total`, `extraFrom`, `known`; `sideBrace`; or `parts`: units and tone; `braces`: from, to, label, below; `unitLabel`), `fill` (Structure only: the coloured boxes without braces or labels; Blank: empty boxes), `size`, `staged` (rows: one row per click, then the brace, then the value of one part in every box (`unitLabel`), then each row's total (`total`, "= share"); a row marked `given` (a known share) shows its total from the first click; single bar: one part per click, then one brace per click; the layout is fixed from the whole bar so nothing moves) |
| `board` | A table to write on | `grid` (`head`, `rows`, `given`), `fill` (Filled in / Structure only / Blank: a control in the player bar; B cycles modes), `size` |
| `questions` | A curated sequence of deck-owned questions | `items: CuratedQuestion[]`, optional `size`, shared `presentation` and `visualiser`; open response, multiple choice, compare claims, find the first error |
| `practice` | Legacy bank references | Read for compatibility; converted to deck copies when editing/exporting. Generator fallback is no longer used. |
| `grid` | Several open-response questions at once | Embedded `items`, `cols`, `size`; the answer replaces the question in its box (all at once with Show answers or `a`, or one box with a 500 ms hold within 8 px); legacy `bank` references remain readable. Rich diagnostics belong in a Questions activity. |
| `generator` | The tool's generator live, like its Whiteboard | `tool`, `preset` (`sub`, `level`, the tool's Question Options), `seed` (the first question, the same every time), `prompt` (blank: the tool's instruction); New question, the shared solution stepper, the answer (keys `n`, `s`, `a`); shared `presentation` (`full`, `whiteboard`, `whiteboard-inverted`) and optional `visualiser` select the saved display layout. In the full-width layout a working step that carries a `graphBuild` (the Solving Trig Equations tool's) draws the graph as it stands at that step beside the steps, the same picture the tool page shows (`WorkedPicture` type `graph`); a workspace layout shows only the answer, so no graph. A maths line with an explicit gap in it (`\\quad`, an equation then its range) is kept on one line, scaled down to fit (`FitLine`). A working that ends with an "Answer:" step saying exactly what the answer box says would print it twice, so `withoutRepeatedAnswer` drops that step (keeping its picture for when the answer is shown) |
| `worksheet` | A book activity: `count` generated questions, numbered | `tool`, `preset`, `count`, `cols`, `seed`; answers on demand (shown in place of each question, like the worksheet), a **500 ms hold** on a cell (within 8 px) flips just that cell, a small regenerate button on each cell (hidden until the activity is pressed, then it fades after a few seconds) replaces that one question with a new one that is not already on the slide, and New set (no Print button); `size` defaults to 60. A worded question shows its maths line at full size, and an instruction shared by every question is shown once above the set |
| `depth` | One of a tool's Depth items, drawn by `DepthSlide` (`src/shared/components/DepthSlide.tsx`), **the one Depth design: the tool page's Depth section draws the same slide** | `tool`, `item` (an id in the tool's `__test.depthItems`); a reference, not a copy, so editing the bank changes every deck that uses it. Show answer turns it to the answer in place; Change card… picks another. Older decks that hold a Depth card as a copied one-question set are turned back into references when read (`normalise.ts`) |

Any element may carry `step` (1, 2, …): it appears on that click when presenting (Next reveals a slide's next click before moving on; Back hides it). The split discussion layout uses it for its fixed order: prompt, key point, question, answer. The ratio table and bar model share the board's Filled in / Structure only / Blank control in the player bar (B cycles). Still to come: the other core representations (place value table, number line, area model, algebra tiles, counters, prime factor tiles), a timer, match-up and sort (listed as "Planned" in the library). The question text of a generator slide is always centred, however long its wording (decided 2026-11-15; before, a worded question over 100 characters was left-aligned). Generator slides draw a ratio-table or fraction-bar working step as its picture (the bar scaled to fit its panel, a long worded question set smaller so it fits); fixed questions cannot hold one yet. <!-- ref -->

## Worksheets pinned to a lesson (`worksheets.ts`, `WorksheetsDialog.tsx`)

A lesson can pin up to three printable worksheets (`MAX_PINNED`). A pinned worksheet is an ordinary book-activity element (`kind: "worksheet"`) with `pin` set (`title`, `paper` A5 or A4, `layout` grid or list, `borders`, `fontPx`), so saving, sharing, undo, copy and export need nothing new. It prints through the same layout engine as the tool pages' Worksheet (`handlePrint`, `src/shared/print.ts`) on an **A5 page** by default: questions, an **answer sheet** (the same boxes in the same places as the question sheet, each with only its answer in it, set larger: `answerScale`), or both. A5 pages print two to an A4 sheet with "Pages per sheet: 2" in the print window. `frozen` keeps the generated questions (working left out, with a `sig` of tool, setup, count and seed) with the deck, so a printed sheet and its answers do not change if a generator does; it applies only while the setup matches, and otherwise the same seed regenerates the same set. <!-- ref -->

- **Pin it:** tick *Pin to the lesson* on a book activity (properties panel: title, page, layout, borders, text size, **New set**, **Print**, **Answer sheet**), or Add slide → **Worksheet**, which starts a pinned slide and opens the generator setup.
- **Print it:** File → **Worksheets…** lists the pinned sheets with Print, Answer sheet and Both.
- **Getting to them:** a **Worksheets** button sits in the top bar of both the player and the editor (shown only when the lesson has pinned sheets) and opens the same window as File → Worksheets…; on a worksheet slide the player's bottom bar also has **Print sheet** and **Answer sheet** buttons.
- **On the slide:** a pinned worksheet can show **the answers** (`view: "key"`, the Worksheet layout's default): numbered answers hidden until `a`, the cue to hand the sheet out. Showing the questions (`view` absent) draws the book activity, without its New set button so the slide always matches the paper. <!-- ref -->
- A hand-written sheet is a pinned worksheet whose `frozen` set is written by hand (worded questions, `working: []`, `sig` copied from the sheet's tool, preset, count and seed): it prints and shows its answers exactly as written, until the seed or setup is changed (then the generator takes over). `specs/decks/_check/ratio-w3-build.py` is the pattern. A worksheet carries no scaffold; the questions fill the page and pupils answer in their books.
- **Textbook sheet** (`src/decks/textbookSheet.ts`, `S.textbookSheet`; added 2026-11-17 after the Corbett Maths standard-form sheet): a pinned worksheet element with `textbook` set, for questions that are only numbers to read and answer in a book. A sheet is a title, an optional note and numbered questions (`firstQuestion` starts the numbering above 1, so one worksheet can run across two sheets; `few: true` on a question allows fewer than five parts), each an instruction and parts (a), (b), (c) ... (a to z, then aa) in four columns (`cols`), with a maths string and an answer for every part. It prints on A4 as one page of questions and one of answers (File → Worksheets, or the player's Print sheet and Answer sheet), and the slide draws the same layout, sized to fill, with `a` (or the button) swapping the answers into the same places. `textbookProblems` is run by the deck check (at least five parts a question, an answer for each part, every string renders). The numbers are written by hand and checked outside the deck; `specs/decks/standard-form.build.ts` is the pattern (the data is in `specs/decks/_check/standard-form-converting.json`). The Editor's Configure button does not apply to one yet. <!-- ref -->
- **Venn sheet** (`src/decks/vennSheet.ts`, `S.vennSheet`; `vennWorksheet` in `src/shared/printables.ts`): a pinned worksheet element with `vennSheet` set, for the Venn, HCF and LCM lesson. Each item is just two numbers (`a`, `b`), so the tiles come from `splitPrimes` and a printed Venn cannot be wrong; `placed` lists the parts (`left`, `middle`, `right`) whose tiles are already printed (the fade: the first Venns carry more), `factors` (default on) prints the two factorisations beside it, `lines` adds "HCF =" and "LCM =" lines to fill in. A4 pages made to be printed scaled to A5: **three Venns to a page, at most nine a sheet** (`MAX_VENN_ITEMS`; the deck check fails more). **`grid: true` is the tall page**: four Venns to an A4 page in a 2 × 2 grid, each cell the Venn on top, the two factorisations under it, then a working line each for the HCF and the LCM (dividing lines, not boxes), **at most twelve a sheet** (`MAX_VENN_GRID_ITEMS`, three pages); the slide shows twelve four across and three down. The drawing is the slide piece's, as a static SVG (`vennItemSvg`, same `layoutCards`, colours and sizes), so slide, editor and paper agree. The slide shows the Venns in a grid with `a` (or the button) filling them in; File → Worksheets prints questions, answer pages (every tile placed, lines filled) or both. `vennSheet.test.ts` covers the page count, the tiles on each page, the escaping and the deck check. <!-- ref -->
- **Why it is not the grid sheet:** the grid worksheet prints numbered boxes in a grid of cells, one instruction above the set and a box to write in; a textbook sheet has several questions on one page, each with its own instruction and lettered parts, and nothing to write in.

## Rectilinear diagrams and authored worksheets

**Deck data and the live server (2026-11-17, updated 2026-11-20).** Deck data is stored in the shared database behind `/api/decks`, and the live server checks every deck with the code on `main`. Since 2026-11-20 that check is tolerant: it checks structure and size, and keeps a question or layout that fails *only* because it uses a value a newer site adds (a question `format` it has not been taught, a rectilinear shape or `version` it does not know), listing it in the push reply (`unchecked`, printed by `npm run deck -- push`). Anything else malformed is still refused (`classifyQuestion` in `api/_questions.ts` and `isNewerRectilinear`: "ok", "unfamiliar" or "invalid"). On the site, an element it cannot draw (an unfamiliar question, an element kind it does not know, or one that throws while drawing) shows "This needs a newer version of the site; it will appear once its code is merged." (`src/decks/newerCode.tsx`) while the rest of the slide carries on, and `deck check` reports it as a warning. So deck content can be pushed before the code that draws it is merged; the content appears properly once that code is deployed. Code that changes the data format should still merge first when it can (a small merge of its own), and a branch's preview deployment is behind the Vercel login, so a push to it needs a bypass secret (pathway `docs/pathways/delivery-workflow.md`, C6).

The generator picker includes **Perimeter & Area → Rectilinear Shapes** (its stable sub-tool id remains `compound`). Live slides retain the tool's `Drawing` and per-step focus, use the shared `ShapeDiagram`, and keep found lengths visible while advancing the solution. A stable seed chooses the first question; New question resets working and answers. Send to a deck honours the same sub-tool limit; diagram questions offer generator/whiteboard/book activities, while fixed chosen dimensions are authored in Skill practice. Other picture tools remain excluded. Full-width and workspace layouts both display the shape. Board length labels are enlarged independently of the unchanged worksheet labels; rectilinear levels default to reduced labelling. <!-- ref -->

In Skill practice, **Add rectilinear shape** creates a portable dimension-based question. Its controls choose L, T, a three- or four-step staircase, a cross, a capital I, U, N, comb or frame, overall dimensions and cut-out sizes (whole centimetres), rotation/reflection, split-and-add or whole-minus-cut-outs, reduced labelling and support. L-shapes can mark one missing length for addition or subtraction; the working finds it before area, and helper labels do not give it away. T-shapes use equal side cut-outs; a cross and a capital I use equal notches (the I is a stem between two flanges: `shape: "I"`, width, height, notch width `cutWidth`, flange thickness `cutHeight`). Staircases use equal steps unless the spec carries `stepWidths` and `stepHeights` (two of each for `stairs3`, three for `stairs4`; the amount each step goes in and the height of each step but the last), which gives steps of different sizes; these are set in the data, not in the panel. **`insideLengths: true`** draws one of the two lengths that meet at each 270° corner inside the shape (the fewest, longest ones, and never two that meet at a 90° corner), so neighbouring labels do not crowd; every other length stays outside (`DEdge.inside`, `src/decks/rectilinear.ts`, `dimensionPlan`). Invalid dimensions disable Apply; dimensions, diagram, answer and canonical working update together. `api/_rectilinear.ts` validates the versioned specification; `src/decks/rectilinear.ts` builds through the live tool's `buildCompound` and independently checks area from the dimensions. `Q.rectilinear(id, spec)` is the script authoring entry point. No picture is serialized as HTML. <!-- ref -->

A completed shape set offers **Make printable worksheet** while fewer than three worksheets are pinned. This saves the practice draft and inserts an independent authored copy on a new worksheet slide. Double-click that worksheet (or Configure) to edit its fixed questions. Each question chooses **Split shown and calculation prompts**, **Split shown, working space**, or **Independent, working space**. Supplied splits label internal component dimensions that have no directly aligned boundary label. The `authored` questions remain fixed when other generator settings change; they are validated on save and preserved by copy/export/import. <!-- ref -->

File → Worksheets prints the questions, worked answers, or both. Authored shape sheets print as A4 pages made to be shrunk two to a sheet (`shapeWorksheet` in `shared/printables.ts`): one page per kind of question (partition, area by splitting, internal lengths, cut-outs; four questions to a page, laid out 2 × 2) with a heading, a Name line and a one-line instruction, the picture large and the working beneath it. A scaffolded question is laid out as numbered steps (find the lengths you need, find each rectangle, add them up; or whole rectangle, part cut out, take it away). Lengths in each picture are sized to about 13pt on A4 (`printFontScale` in `rectilinearPrint.tsx`, so a tall narrow shape is not left with tiny labels), working lines are 12.5pt, and nothing is under 10.5pt, so it stays readable at 70%. The printed answer pages match the question order and include the method. Generated rectilinear worksheets and older generated/frozen worksheets keep `diagramPracticeSheet` (six to an A4 page, A5 uses two). <!-- ref -->

Verification: `npm run smoke:rectilinear-deck` uses `SMOKE_URL`, `PLAYWRIGHT_PATH` and `CHROMIUM_PATH` like the other deck checks. It isolates all deck API calls from live data and checks invalid edits, save/reload, worksheet creation, diagram reveal, phone/tablet/laptop presentation and printing through the Worksheets dialog. Unit coverage lives in `rectilinearDeck.test.ts`.

## Layouts (`src/decks/layouts.ts`)

A layout is a function that returns a pre-arranged slide of ordinary elements: title, learning objective, section divider, summary, key vocabulary, key definition, two columns, board, skill practice, quickfire block, Depth card, split discussion, retrieval starter, exit ticket, blank. Families: Lesson (structure), Language, Teach, Practice, Depth, Discussion, Retrieval. `LAYOUTS` lists each with its sample content and when to use it; the editor's **Add slide** and the library both read it, and the built decks are written with the same functions (so a built deck and a teacher's slide are the same kind of thing). Add a layout only when a lesson needs it, after checking none in the family does the job.

## Built decks, edits and the teacher's own decks (`src/decks/store.ts`)

- **Decks are data** (the shared copy; `src/decks/data/<id>.json` is its backup in the repo; the deck holds its own `tool` and `toolPath`). The shared copy (cloud) is the master, because Harry edits there: Claude runs `npm run deck -- pull <id>` first, changes the JSON by slide id, then `npm run deck -- push <id>` (`npm run deck -- list` shows what is shared). Push only a deck you have just pulled. **Decks are not bundled into the app (since 2026-11-11):** a device lists only the decks it has synced from the shared copy (the Decks page, the editor list, a tool's menu and the dashboard each sync on opening), so a brand-new device offline shows none. The repo JSON is a backup and what the tests (`src/tests/snapshotDecks.ts`) and `npm run deck` scripts read. Deleting a deck in the editor deletes the shared copy and this device's copy (there is no "Reset to the original" any more); a repo snapshot of a deleted deck is removed by hand. Slide ids are stable (`<deck>-<slug>`). <!-- ref -->
- **Edits are saved on this device** (localStorage) as the teacher makes them: an edited built deck is saved whole under `mt-deck:<id>` and **Reset** returns it to the original; a teacher's own deck (`my-…`) is listed in `mt-decks-mine`. Question copies are embedded in each deck. Editing them changes that deck only and participates in deck undo. The shared question library (a separate store of a teacher's own questions, `mt-deck-bank` on the device and a `questions` table on the server) was removed on 2026-11-20: nothing wrote to it and the live copy was empty; an old `practice` or `grid` slide that points at a question by id finds it in the built-in bank only, and new questions live inside their slide. Ordinary authoring has no library browse or save action. <!-- ref -->
- **Export** materializes legacy question references and downloads the complete question content, including misconception descriptions and teaching guidance, as JSON; **Import slides** appends a file's slides to the open deck. Copy / paste (Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V, or the slide's Copy button and "Paste a copied slide") works between slides and between decks.
- **Shared between devices** (`src/decks/cloud.ts`, `api/decks.ts`, `api/_decks.ts`): every save is sent in the background (a moment after the last change) to `/api/decks`, which keeps the decks in **Neon Postgres** (since 2026-11-11; schema `maths`, created on first use by `api/_db.ts`; every read and write is in `api/_deckStore.ts`): `decks` (the deck as JSON, its summary columns for the index, and a `version`), `deck_versions`, `published_decks`. A save reads the deck with its version, merges, and writes only if the version has not moved (retried if another save got in first); the list of decks is a query on the summary columns. The connection is `DATABASE_URL` (the Neon integration on the `maths-tools` project; the one database serves Production, Preview and Development, so a Preview deployment writes real data). Decks used to be Vercel Blob files (`decks/<id>.json`, `decks-index.json`, `history/`, `published/`); `api/migrate-decks.ts` copied them across once (temporary, safe to re-run) and is deleted once the move is confirmed. Opening the deck list or a deck pulls any copy newer than the device's (`syncFromCloud`, comparing the deck's own `updatedAt`); nothing is pulled while editing. **The first open of a deck waits for the shared copy** ("Loading the latest copy…", at most `LIVE_WAIT_MS` = 4 s, then the device's copy is used), so an older copy is not shown and then swapped; this also covers opening straight into `?edit=1`, and the deck list (`/decks`) waits the same way ("Loading the latest decks…") before drawing its cards. Header `x-admin-key` as for `/api/tool-notes`. **Shared version history** (the rollback for the live copy): before a save changes the stored deck, the service keeps the deck as it stood in `deck_versions`, at most one every `SNAPSHOT_GAP_MS` (10 min, so a spell of typing makes one) and the latest `MAX_SNAPSHOTS` (40); a save that changes nothing makes none. `GET /api/decks?history=<id>` lists them (newest first) and `&key=<key>` returns one; File → **Version history** shows them above the device's own, and Restore applies the old copy as an ordinary save (so it merges like any save, and Undo reverses it). A failed snapshot does not fail the save. Deleting a deck does not make a snapshot, and its earlier snapshots are kept. Tests: `decksHistory.test.ts`. A deck over 4 MB (large pictures) is refused with a message. Where the service is not reachable (the local dev server, no connection) the decks keep working on the device and the status line says so. **The device holds few decks (2026-11-20):** the Decks page, the tool menu, the dashboard and the "Add to deck" dialogs list decks from the shared list (`GET /api/decks` returns, per deck, its title, summary, where it is filed, tool, slides by kind and first slide: `listIndex` in `api/_deckStore.ts`), laid out by `deckSummaries()` in `src/decks/store.ts`, which puts this device's newer copy over the shared entry. A deck is fetched when opened (`ensureDeck`, or `syncFromCloud(id)`), a whole-list check refreshes only the decks the device already holds, and after each sync the device keeps the 8 most recently opened or saved (`trimDevice`), dropping an older one (with its saved versions and base) only when the shared copy is at least as new, so unsent work is never lost. If storage is still full a save frees the saved versions and base copies of other decks first and then reports "Storage full" rather than "Updated". <!-- ref -->

## The player (`DeckPage.tsx`, `SlideCanvas.tsx`)

Arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Enter and Space move slides (the keys a USB presentation clicker sends, read in `clickerKeys.ts`; `.` blanks the screen, `F5` starts full screen, `k` shows a key test readout); Home and End jump; `j` opens the jump list; full screen. Curated activities have **Previous question**, **Next question**, a question count and an explicit end of sequence. Answers start hidden on every question. **Show working** moves the question smoothly from the centre to the right and opens the unfolding solution on the left. Previous / Next step, with **Show all / Step-by-Step** between them, navigate its numbered timeline (the step count sits beside the Worked solution heading); earlier steps dim, the newest is highlighted, and the final answer has a green A marker. **Show answer** (`a`) jumps to that final solution; `w` advances one beat; The player has no teacher-guidance button and no More menu; guidance stays in the editor's Teaching details. Choices and worked lines can be selected before the reveal. Curated sequences never fall through to a generator; add a generator activity separately. Answer notation belongs to the authored content (Estimation explicitly includes approximation). Curated questions, generator questions and workspace previews share `src/shared/components/SolutionStepper.tsx`. The question stays in the same DOM node during the move. Hiding the working returns it to the centre; changing question clears solution progress. Long solutions follow the newest step inside their own column, keeping question and navigation in place. A narrow popup stacks question above working; the 16:9 classroom canvas keeps the split. Reduced-motion preferences disable the drift and fades. Ink clears on a question or slide change. <!-- ref -->
