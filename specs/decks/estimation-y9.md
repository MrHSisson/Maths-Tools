# Lesson plan: Estimation, Year 9

**Status:** implemented (Depth cards in `EstimationDepth.ts`, deck in `src/decks/data/estimation.json`) · **Tool:** Estimation (and Rounding for the 1 s.f. practice) · **Class:** Year 9, need lots of practice, calculators available

**Objective:** estimate the answer to a calculation by rounding each number to 1 significant figure first.

**Starting point:** they have met significant figures. Slips to plan for: losing a zero when multiplying rounded numbers, dropping the last digit instead of rounding, rounding to the wrong place, rounding in two steps (649 → 650 → 700).

**Shape:** **one deck, worked through from the start and picked up wherever the lesson ends.** It is meant for about two lessons, with no fixed timings. Section dividers are the pick-up points, and the first slide after the Fractions divider is a recap, so it also works as the start of lesson 2. Within each section: a worked board, an authored set, then a generator slide for repeat practice. Edge cases are few and sit at the end of a set, never in a row. Each worked board ends with the calculator answer beside the estimate, so students can check.

Levels follow the tool: whole numbers = Level 1, fractions (whole numbers over a fraction bar) = Level 3, decimals = Level 2, so fractions come before decimals. Level 1 already avoids the awkward cases (a leading 5, 95 and over), so its generator never reduces morale.

Question sets have five fixed questions with working that matches the generator.

## Depth questions to author (new, for the Estimation tool)

Each follows the Depth rules: a title that doesn't give the answer away, every wrong option names its misconception, a teacher note, and numbers the deck's own questions don't use. The tool's twelve existing cards stay available, and I'd reuse *Who estimated well?*, *Dividing by a decimal* and *A fraction to estimate* as extras.

| Section | Title | What it probes | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rounding 649 | Rounding in two steps (649 → 650 → 700) instead of looking at the second digit only. Answer 600. | Who is right? |
| 2 | The calculator slip | Kofi types 33 × 58 and gets 191.4. Use an estimate (about 1,800) to show it must be wrong. | Open, with a worked reason |
| 3 | Stopping part-way | A student rounds the top but leaves 28 + 21 as 49 in the denominator. Round every number first. | Find the first error |
| 3 | Which one is it? | Subtraction estimate misleads: 502 − 498 estimates as 0. When is an estimate close and when is it not? | Always, sometimes or never |
| 4 | Does the size make sense? | Place value of the answer: 0.48 × 0.062 is about 0.03; choose from 0.3, 0.03, 0.003, 3. | Multiple choice |
| 4 | Over or under? | Multiplying with one number rounded up and one down: is the estimate bigger or smaller than the exact answer? | Always, sometimes or never |
| 4 | Make an estimate of 0.3 | Build calculations with an estimate of 0.3, using decimals. | Open (extension) |
