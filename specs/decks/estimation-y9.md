# Lesson plan: Estimation, Year 9

**Status:** draft, for Harry to review · **Tool:** Estimation (and Rounding for the 1 s.f. practice) · **Class:** Year 9, need lots of practice, calculators available

**Objective:** estimate the answer to a calculation by rounding each number to 1 significant figure first.

**Starting point:** they have met significant figures. Common slips to plan for: losing a zero when multiplying rounded numbers, dropping the last digit instead of rounding, rounding to the wrong place, rounding in two steps (649 → 650 → 700).

**Shape:** three lessons of about 60 minutes (assumed; tell me if shorter), one deck each, always in the same order: rounding practice, then estimating whole numbers (+ − × ÷), then fractions, then decimals. Questions climb gently. Edge cases are few and sit at the end of a set, never in a row. Every worked example ends with the calculator answer beside the estimate, so the estimate is something they can check. Generator slides give repeat practice after each authored set.

Levels follow the tool: whole numbers = Level 1, fractions (whole numbers over a fraction bar) = Level 3, decimals = Level 2, so fractions come before decimals. Level 1 already avoids the awkward cases (a leading 5, 95 and over), so the generator never reduces morale.

## Depth questions to author (new, for the Estimation tool)

Each follows the Depth rules: a title that doesn't give the answer away, every wrong option names its misconception, a teacher note, and numbers the deck's own questions don't use. The tool's twelve existing cards stay available, and I'd reuse *Who estimated well?*, *Dividing by a decimal* and *A fraction to estimate* as extras.

| Lesson | Title | What it probes | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rounding 649 | Rounding in two steps (649 → 650 → 700) instead of looking at the second digit only. Answer 600. | Who is right? |
| 1 | The calculator slip | Kofi types 33 × 58 and gets 191.4. Use an estimate (about 1,800) to show it must be wrong. | Open, with a worked reason |
| 2 | Which one is it? | Subtraction estimate misleads: 502 − 498 estimates as 0; when is an estimate close and when is it not? | Always, sometimes or never |
| 2 | Stopping part-way | A student rounds the top but leaves 28 + 21 as 49 in the denominator. Round every number first. | Find the first error |
| 3 | Does the size make sense? | Place value of the answer: 0.48 × 0.062 is about 0.03; choose from 0.3, 0.03, 0.003, 3. | Multiple choice |
| 3 | Over or under? | Multiplying with one number rounded up and one down: is the estimate bigger or smaller than the exact answer? | Always, sometimes or never |
| 3 | Make an estimate of 0.3 | Build calculations with an estimate of 0.3, using decimals. | Open (extension) |
