# Factors, common factors and HCF

**Status:** implemented (deck `src/decks/data/factors-hcf-systematic-listing.json`, 17 slides, built 2026-11-13 and revised 2026-11-14 after Harry's first look; awaiting his next check)
**Class:** Set 6, low attainers · **Teaching time:** one lesson · **Calculator:** Casio fx-85
**Deck:** `factors-hcf-systematic-listing` · **Strand:** Number · **Tool:** none (every question is authored)

## Agreed approach (proposed)

Follows `multiples-systematic-listing`: systematic listing, hand-authored questions, no generator, no formula. Sequence as Harry set it: **list the factors of a number → find the common factors of two numbers → identify the HCF** (the highest of the common factors). Calculators are in pupils' hands, so the deck teaches exactly how to use one to find factors, with the key presses shown on the slide.

**The calculator method (taught explicitly, slide 3 to 5):**
1. A number is a factor if it divides exactly: type `n ÷ d =`. **A whole number on the display means d is a factor; anything else (a fraction such as 24/5 on the fx-85's natural display, or 4.8 after S⇔D) means it is not.**
2. Work through d = 1, 2, 3, ... in order. Each exact division gives a **pair**: d and the answer are both factors.
3. **Stop when the answer is smaller than the number you divided by** (24 ÷ 5 = 4.8 is smaller than 5, so stop). A repeated answer (36 ÷ 6 = 6) is written once.
4. To change the divisor without retyping, press the left arrow after `=` to bring the sum back and change the divisor (exact key wording to be checked against the pupils' fx-85 before building).
5. For common factors, list the smaller number's factors, then **test each on the other number** (18 ÷ 4 = 4.5, so 4 is not a common factor of 12 and 18).

Same house style as the multiples deck: no starter, no Level labels, worked numbers never reappear in a question of their own half, no pair repeats, every book answer is different, edge cases sit at the end. LCM is not taught, but the HCF discussion uses the common-multiple idea from last lesson as the trap.

**Help, and weaning it off (Harry, 2026-11-14, second version: he liked the scaffold).** Whiteboard numbers are bigger than the book numbers so a factor is not seen at a glance. In each section the three whiteboards climb: **1. blanks** (the pairs partly completed, a partner left as a box), **2. the number of pairs in brackets** ("60 (6 pairs)", "40 (4 pairs) and 56 (4 pairs)"), **3. just the numbers** (a friendly number: 30; 20 and 50; 20 and 30). Whiteboard questions are kept short (under 170 characters, split into short lines) so the player centres them without any shared-code change, and the common-factors and HCF whiteboards use a smaller text size. Forms:

- **Blanks:** `1 × 48, 2 × 24, 3 × __, 4 × 12, 6 × __`, at most three pairs to a line; for two numbers a labelled line for each (`30: 1 × 30, 2 × __ ...`).
- **Brackets:** the number of factor pairs after each number.
- **Hint line (book work):** a small line under the number(s).
- **None:** the number and the instruction only.

## Question ladder and checks

**Ladder:** two things climb together: the numbers (whiteboards 30 to 72, book sets 28 to 100 for listing and 14 to 160 for common factors and HCF) and the support falling away (see "Help, and weaning it off"). Each book set: six hinted questions that grow gently, then three stretch questions with no hint: listing 100 (a square, 5 pairs), 84 and 96 (6 pairs each); common factors 54 and 90 (6 common), 70 and 105 (4 common, via 5 and 7), 48 and 80 (5 common); HCF 75 and 100 (25), 52 and 78 (26), 96 and 160 (32). Every book HCF answer is different.

**Traps** (each has a question where the wrong method gives a different, tempting answer):

| Trap | Where | Wrong-method result |
|---|---|---|
| Leaving out 1 and the number itself | list-1 to 3, book-1 to 9 | 30 listed as 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15 |
| Stopping too early (not reaching the pair partner) | list-2 (60), book-6 (80) | 80 listed as 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10 |
| Assuming a factor in a gap without testing it (or skipping a divisor) | list-1 (5 and 7 for 48), book-1 (3 for 28) | 5 added to the factors of 48 |
| Listing a multiple instead of a factor | list-3 (30), exit-1 | 30 listed as 30, 60, 90 |
| A square's middle factor written twice or missed | book-7 (100) | 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 10, 20, ... |
| Giving only one common factor (the biggest, or the first) | cf-1 to 3, cfb-1 to 9 | 20 and 50: 10 only |
| Forgetting 1 is always a common factor | cfb-6 (14 and 15) | "no common factors" |
| HCF read as a common factor (not the highest) | hcf-1 to 3, hcfb-1 to 9 | 20 and 30: 5 or 2 |
| HCF confused with the common multiple from last lesson | slide 14 (12 and 18) | 36 instead of 6 |
