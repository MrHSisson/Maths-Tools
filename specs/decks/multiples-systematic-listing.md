# Multiples and common multiples

**Status:** implemented
**Class:** Set 6 (year not specified) · **Teaching time:** 45 minutes after a separate TT starter
**Deck:** `multiples-systematic-listing` · **Strand:** Number · **Tool:** none

## Agreed approach

Systematic listing only: label two lists, start with each number itself, list positive multiples in order, extend until a match appears, circle it in both lists, then state the LCM. Times-table grids are optional support; repeated addition provides a route when recall is insecure. All questions are hand-authored. No starter slide, factorisation, factor trees, formula or generator.

Harry confirmed the group can list multiples of any number by repeated addition; insecure multiplication recall does not justify single-digit examples. Listing therefore uses two-digit numbers, and LCM pairs are selected for short searches. Independent book questions are fresh, including when pairs are reversed.

After review, Harry requested two distinct book activities: nine first-five-multiples questions before introducing common multiples, followed by common-multiple/LCM work only. The nine-question activity is an explicit exception to the usual twelve-question book layout. The repeated-addition model uses arrows between consecutive multiples, with +15 beneath each arrow.

| 7–12 | 5 | Six whiteboard questions: partial lists → repeated-addition prompt → independent listing. |
| 12–20 | 6 | Nine fresh first-five-multiples questions in books, 3 × 3. Numbers 5, 8, 12, 13, 22, 26, 34, 38, 45 (low starters first). Check answers in the last two minutes. |
| 20–25 | 7–8 | Define common multiple. List multiples of 18 and 27; circle 54 in both. Extend to show 108 is another common multiple. |

| 29–34 | 11 | Five guided LCM questions with fading scaffolds. |
| 34–42 | 12 | Twelve fresh LCM questions only, 3 × 4. Questions 1–4 have list starters; 5–12 are independent. Completion is not expected; check answers in the last two minutes. |
| 42–45 | 13 | Three common-multiple/LCM exit questions. No return to standalone listing. |

## Question ladder and checks

**No-repeat rule (revised after review).** A number used in a worked example (listing model 15 and together 11, 21, 30; common-multiple model 18 and 27; LCM model 12 and 16) never reappears as a base later (except 12, used deliberately as a low listing starter). Every listing base (model, together, practice, book work) is different; every LCM base in the guided and book sets is different; the exit ticket reuses only four guided bases (20, 24, 50, 33) in new pairs. The listing half and the LCM half are separate activities, so a listing-book number may reappear in an LCM pair (e.g. 22, 26, 34, 38, 45 in the nine-question book work, which also has 5, 8, 12 and 13 as low starters, never in the same pair; 24 and 35 from the whiteboard practice; 30 from the together table in the nested 10 and 30 pair). No pair repeats, reversed or not, and no answer repeats within a set. Lists may run to eight entries but only one does (25 and 40, guided question 3); most searches need two to five. The nested pair is 10 and 30 (book question 4, supported); question 12 (26 and 39) is the stretch, next common multiple 156.
