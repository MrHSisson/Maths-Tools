# Multiples and common multiples

**Status:** implemented
**Class:** Set 6 (year not specified) · **Teaching time:** 45 minutes after a separate TT starter
**Deck:** `multiples-systematic-listing` · **Strand:** Number · **Tool:** none

## Agreed approach

Low-ability group, one lesson, kept short (revised after Harry's review). Systematic listing only: label two lists, start with each number itself, list positive multiples in order, extend until a match appears, circle it. Times-table grids are optional support; repeated addition is the route when recall is insecure. All questions are hand-authored. No factorisation, formula or generator. **LCM is not taught in this lesson** (a later lesson, possibly with calculators); the common multiple the pupils find is simply the first one they meet.

The lesson has two halves of the same shape: direct instruction, a few whiteboard examples, then nine book questions, easiest first.

## Teaching sequence

| Slides | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Title; definition of a multiple. |
| 3–4 | Direct instruction: first five multiples of 15 with an arrow and +15 under it for each new term; then a structure-only table (11 given, 21 together, 30 tried). |
| 5 | Three whiteboard questions: two partly completed lists, then one independent list (14, 17, 19). |
| 6 | Nine first-five-multiples questions in books, 3 × 3, easiest first (5, 8, 12 low starters). |
| 7–8 | Definition of a common multiple; model with 4 and 6: circle 12 in both lists, then extend to show 24 is another. |
| 9 | Discussion: Mei says the only common multiple of 4 and 6 is 12. Key point: 24 and 36 are too, and the list never stops; then is 24 a common multiple of 2 and 3? |
| 10 | Three whiteboard questions: 3 and 5 with starters, then 2 and 7, then 8 and 10 (compact: question, lists, circled answer). |
| 11 | Nine common-multiple questions in books, 3 × 3: questions 1–6 find a common multiple; questions 7–9 find the first two, with no list starters anywhere. |
| 12 | Exit ticket: first five multiples of 9; circle the common multiple in the 2 and 5 lists (10); find a common multiple of 3 and 7 (21). |

The exit ticket takes the last 3 minutes; trim the book work if time is short.

## Question ladder and checks

Rules: a worked-example number never reappears in a question in its own half; no pair repeats; every base in the book work is different; the listing and common-multiple halves are separate, so a listing number may return in a pair but never beside its listing partner. Lists may run to eight entries; only 9 and 12 (question 8) does.
