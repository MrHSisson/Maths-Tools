# Deck plan: Fractional powers

**Status:** implemented (39 slides; negative fractional powers added at the end; second letters board removed after review)
**Deck id:** `fractional-powers` · **Tool:** `indices` (`/indices`) · **Built for:** about two lessons, picked up at the dividers
**Idea:** pupils already know the index laws. Use them to *discover* what a fractional power must mean, then practise it numerically, algebraically and with the laws.

## Arc

| # | Section (divider) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recap: the index laws | Quickfire on positive, negative and zero powers, then "why is $a^0 = 1$?" so the class sees that the laws *decide* what a new power means |
| 2 | What is $a^{\frac12}$? | $a^{\frac12} \times a^{\frac12} = a^1$, so $a^{\frac12}$ is the number that multiplies by itself to give $a$: the square root. Same for $a^{\frac13}$ and the cube root |
| 3 | Powers like $\frac23$ | Power of a power: $a^{\frac23} = (a^{\frac13})^2$, so root first, then power |
| 4 | Fractional powers of numbers | $16^{\frac12}$, $8^{\frac13}$, $125^{\frac23}$, then practice |
| 5 | Fractional powers of letters | $x^{\frac23} = (\sqrt[3]{x})^2$ and the other way round, then practice |
| 6 | Fractional powers and the laws | Multiply, divide, power of a power with fractional indices |

## Fixed question sets (answers checked at build)

**Slide 16, numbers (7).** Working follows the tool: "The denominator is the root and the numerator is the power. Take the root first".

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $36^{\frac12}$ | $6$ |
| 2 | $64^{\frac13}$ | $4$ |
| 3 | $81^{\frac14}$ | $3$ |
| 4 | $9^{\frac32}$ | $27$ |
| 5 | $64^{\frac23}$ | $16$ |
| 6 | $16^{\frac34}$ | $8$ |
| 7 | $4^{\frac52}$ | $32$ |

**Slide 21, index → root form (6).** $x^{\frac14}$ → $\sqrt[4]{x}$ · $y^{\frac15}$ → $\sqrt[5]{y}$ · $x^{\frac23}$ → $(\sqrt[3]{x})^2$ · $a^{\frac32}$ → $(\sqrt{a})^3$ · $y^{\frac34}$ → $(\sqrt[4]{y})^3$ · $m^{\frac52}$ → $(\sqrt{m})^5$

**Slide 22, root → index form (6).** $\sqrt{b}$ → $b^{\frac12}$ · $\sqrt[3]{t}$ → $t^{\frac13}$ · $(\sqrt{p})^5$ → $p^{\frac52}$ · $(\sqrt[5]{k})^2$ → $k^{\frac25}$ · $(\sqrt[3]{n})^4$ → $n^{\frac43}$ · $\sqrt[4]{c^3}$ → $c^{\frac34}$

**Slide 28, laws (7).**

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $x^{\frac12} \times x^{\frac32}$ | $x^2$ |
| 2 | $a^{\frac13} \times a^{\frac23}$ | $a$ |
| 3 | $y^{\frac52} \div y^{\frac12}$ | $y^2$ |
| 4 | $(x^{\frac12})^4$ | $x^2$ |
| 5 | $(a^{\frac23})^6$ | $a^4$ |

The last one has unlike denominators (the tool's "Different denominators" type), one edge case at the end of the set.

## The new graphics

The site's own pictures cannot be dropped onto a slide yet, so each graphic is **built from ordinary slide elements** (tiles, brackets, arrows, maths) with click order, so you can move or edit any part in the editor. House rule from the indices pictures: **the same base is always the same tile**.

| Graphic | What it shows |
|---|---|
| 1 | Two equal tiles $a^{\frac12}$ side by side, multiplication sign, equals one tile $a$. A bracket underneath adds the indices: $\frac12 + \frac12 = 1$ |
| 2 | Three equal tiles $a^{\frac13}$, $\frac13 + \frac13 + \frac13 = 1$, equals $a$ |
| 3 | A route: $a$ → cube root → square → $a^{\frac23}$, with $(a^{\frac13})^2$ written under it and the power-of-a-power law $\frac13 \times 2 = \frac23$ |
